We are creating an expansion set of keyword cards for the game So Clover!

In So Clover, players create one-word clues connecting pairs of keywords.

Rate the following words from 1 (worst) to 10 (best) based on how suitable they are for the game in the following aspects:

# Association Diversity

How many different ideas, concepts, domains, or topics the word naturally connects to. Prefer words that support many independent associations in different domains rather than many very similar associations.

High ratings:

connect broadly across many concepts
participate in many semantic neighborhoods
naturally combine with many other words

Low ratings:

isolated or narrow
few meaningful associations
difficult to connect naturally

Provide the following ratings:
* semantic/category: Associations based on meaning, taxonomy, properties, or conceptual similarity. These are the most straightforward "related concept" connections. Examples: dog → animal, pet, bark, leash; apple → fruit, food, tree; ocean → water, fish, wave
* functional: Associations based on what something does, how it is used, or what it interacts with. Examples: hammer → building, nails, wood; key → lock, open; oven → bake, rack
* multiple senses: Associations arising because the word has multiple legitimate meanings. Examples: pitch → melody, baseball, sales, tar; crane → bird, construction; match → competition, fire, pair
* metaphorical/symbolic: Associations where the word represents broader ideas, concepts, emotions, or relationships beyond its literal meaning These associations may arise from figurative analogy, abstract comparison, or culturally recognized symbolic meaning. Examples: fire → passion, energy, intensity; anchor → emotional stability; bridge → connection between people; dove → peace; heart → love; scales → justice
* idioms/phrases: Associations arising from common expressions or fixed phrases. Examples: ice → break (the ice); glass → (glass) ceiling
* wordplay: Associations based on pronunciation, rhyme, puns, spelling, alliteration, or linguistic play rather than meaning. Examples: knight → night; star → super, fish; desert → dessert
* visual: Associations based on what the word visually evokes. These often rely on mental pictures, shapes, colors, scenes, or aesthetics. Examples: pyramid → triangular structure; jungle → dense green vegetation; mirror → reflection
* emotional: Associations tied to feelings, moods, or emotional tone. Examples: funeral → sadness; birthday → joy; home → comfort
* cultural/historical: Associations arising from shared media, pop culture, religion, memes, brands, folklore, societal knowledge, historical events, people, or movements. Examples: apple → technology, company, phone; romeo → romance; matrix → trinity, pill, AI; titanic → iceberg, lifeboat; berlin → east, west, wall; caesar → rome, emperor, march; revolution → French, American
* overall_associations: Overall bredth, diversity, and flexibility of the word’s associations across different types of clueing routes.

# Gameplay Suitability

* recognizability: How likely the average player is to know and understand the word quickly.
* evocativeness: How strongly the word evokes mental imagery, sensory experiences, emotions, stories, memorable concepts, or vivid situations.
* fun: Overall potential for creating clever moments, surprising connections, memorable gameplay, satisfying social interactions, or interesting player decisions.
* overall_gameplay: Overall rating of Gameplay Suitability.
